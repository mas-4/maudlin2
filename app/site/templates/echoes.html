🔧 How the echoes are found

From 413,186 political articles printed in 1896 (American Stories, Dell et al. 2023, from Chronicling America), 520 were read by a local language model for their claims, each kept only if its quote is in the article. The claims were filed to a copy of today's motif index by the pipeline that files today's claims (the Lab's 🧪 Rip Van Winkle). An echo is a claim filed with confidence to a motif the site shows, and it's shown only once it has been checked against the whole article: by the person who runs the site, or by Claude (Opus 5.5), who sets aside for the person anything close to a slur or a trope, or too close to call. Claims printed in more than one paper were found by matching near-copies across papers, as 1896's exchange papers reprinted each other.
