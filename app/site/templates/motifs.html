    How the index works

    🧩 A motif is a shape a story takes, named so the same story told about other people or in another year lands in the same entry: Pattern of retribution, not one whistleblower's death. The name and the shape are ours; the shape has to be in what the tellers say, not in why the story spreads. A claim can carry several motifs.

    Genre is what kind of thing a motif is, not its topic. Every motif has one:

      {% for g in genres %}
    • {{ g.emoji }} {{ g.name }} ({{ g.count }}): {{ g.about|e }}
      • {% endfor %}

    🗂️ Groups are loose shelves of motifs that go together, across genres (Horserace, Who rules, War motifs); a motif can sit in several. 🪜 Rests on is the one hierarchy: a motif rests on another when it only makes sense given it, as a kind of it or a case, argument or figure that tells it.

    A language model running on our own computer suggests motifs and files claims under them. A person checks each motif before it's shown here: its name, its note, its genre, its groups and the claims in it. The genres' definitions are found as we sort, case by case, and change when a case doesn't fit.
