🧶 Folklore

⚠️ These are rumors, not facts.

Everything on this page is what people are saying, summarized by a language model, not what is true. A claim appearing here does not mean it's true, and many are false. Its genre and motif describe the shape of the story as its tellers tell it, the way a folklorist classifies a legend: they are not truth claims, and they're the same whether people tell the story or argue against it. Where a fact-checker has examined a claim, the check is linked (🔎).

🚧 Work in progress. An experiment in finding the stories people retell in their own words: rumors, legends, beliefs, jokes and sayings, not the news itself. Read from a sample of public Bluesky and Mastodon posts; the labels are a small local language model's best guess and are often wrong.

How this works

Folklore travels as variants: one claim or story told by many unrelated people, each in their own words. Copypasta and coordinated posting travel as the same words. So posts saying the same thing in different words are grouped together (by meaning, with an embedding model), and each group is measured by how many different people posted it and how varied their wording is. Feeds, bots and accounts posting many times an hour are left out.

For each big group, our local language model reads a few versions and says what they share, whether it's retold or just a shared topic, its genre, the recurring motif it's an instance of (in our own motif index). When the posts share articles from outlets we follow, those articles are shown (an exact link to the story); otherwise a link to the day's news appears only when the model, choosing among the closest stories with "none" on offer, picks one. When a fact-checker (PolitiFact, Snopes, FactCheck.org, Lead Stories, Full Fact, NewsGuard, Science Feedback) has examined the same claim in the last 30 days, the check is linked (🔎), chosen the same way.

What's here is the model's summary of what people's posts share. A post is shown only if it was already widely seen ({{ bsky_rule }}), and only embedded from Bluesky itself, so it disappears here if its author deletes it; never from anyone who has asked Bluesky not to show their posts to logged-out visitors, and never from Mastodon. Raw posts are kept 30 days for this research.

🧩 Motif

The recurring shape a story is an instance of, from our own motif index: a list we build as we go, the way Stith Thompson built his Motif-Index of Folk-Literature from the folktales he read, but from today's political talk. Thompson's index (built from folktales, myths and legends) fits modern political rumor poorly, so we don't use it. Each motif is named as a reusable framing, without names or dates, so the same story told about other people or another year lands in the same entry; the model files each claim, and a person refines the entries by hand.

Rumor, conspiracy and subject

Three short label sets from the study of rumor, read by the model from each story as its tellers tell it, never as a judgment of whether it's true:
