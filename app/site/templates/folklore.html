🧶 Folklore and rumors

⚠️ These are rumors, not facts.

Everything on this page is what people are saying, summarized by a language model, not what is true. A claim appearing here does not mean it's true, and many are false. Its motifs, genre and labels describe the shape of the story as its tellers tell it, the way a folklorist classifies a legend: they are not truth claims, and they're the same whether people tell the story or argue against it. Where a fact-checker has examined a claim, the verdict is the fact-checker's: follow the link (🔎) to read it.

How this works

🧶 Retold online. Folklore travels as variants: one claim or story told by many unrelated people, each in their own words. Copypasta and coordinated posting travel as the same words. So posts saying the same thing in different words are grouped together (by meaning, with an embedding model), and each group is measured by how many different people posted it and how varied their wording is. Feeds, bots and accounts posting many times an hour are left out. For each big group, our local language model reads a few versions and says what they share and whether it's retold or just a shared topic. When the posts share articles from outlets we follow, those articles are shown (an exact link to the story); otherwise a link to the day's news appears only when the model, choosing among the closest stories with "none" on offer, picks one.

🎙️ On the shows. Every podcast, call-in and video show we transcribe, right, left and center, is read by our local language model for the claims told on it, and a claim is kept only if the words it's drawn from are really in the transcript and support it. The same claim told on different shows is grouped the way posts are. One host's take isn't folklore; a claim shows here once it's told on two or more shows, or by two or more callers. What callers say is shown only in our words, never their own or who they are.

🔎 Fact-checked. Rumors examined by PolitiFact, FactCheck.org, Snopes, Lead Stories, Full Fact, NewsGuard and Science Feedback in the last {{ check_days }} days, the claim in the model's words. When a narrative told online matches one, the check is linked from its card instead (chosen by the model among the closest, with "none" on offer). "In our social sample" says whether people in the last few days of posts are telling the claim or arguing against it.

What's here is the model's summary of what people say. A post is shown only if it was already widely seen ({{ bsky_rule }}), and only embedded from Bluesky itself, so it disappears here if its author deletes it; never from anyone who has asked Bluesky not to show their posts to logged-out visitors, and never from Mastodon. Raw posts are kept 30 days for this research.

What the labels mean

🧩 Motif

The recurring shape a story is an instance of, from our own motif index: a list we build as we go, the way Stith Thompson built his Motif-Index of Folk-Literature from the folktales he read, but from today's political talk. Each motif is named as a reusable framing, without names or dates, so the same story told about other people or another year lands in the same entry; the model files each claim, and a person refines the entries by hand. Only motifs a person has checked are shown.

Genre

The kind of story a motif is, sorted by a person as the index grows{% if genre_counts %} (so far: {% for g, n in genre_counts %}{{ g|e }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}){% endif %}. A genre belongs to a motif, not to a claim, so a card's genres are those of its motifs, and a card with no checked motif has none yet.

Rumor, conspiracy and subject

Three short label sets from the study of rumor, read by the model from each story as its tellers tell it, never as a judgment of whether it's true (not yet for what's told on the shows):

  • Rumor, by what drives it (Robert Knapp, 1944; Nicholas DiFonzo and Prashant Bordia, 2007): {% for k, note in rumor_classes.items() if k != 'other' %}{{ shape_emoji.get(k, '') }} {{ k }}, {{ note }}{% if not loop.last %}; {% endif %}{% endfor %}.
  • Conspiracy, by the scope of the secret plot it claims (Michael Barkun, 2003): {% for k, note in conspiracy_scopes.items() if k != 'not a conspiracy' %}{{ shape_emoji.get(k, '') }} {{ k }}, {{ note }}{% if not loop.last %}; {% endif %}{% endfor %}.
  • Subject: a broad family (crime and danger, health, celebrities, government…), our own list, after the way urban-legend collections such as Jan Harold Brunvand's sort legends into chapters.

Told by counts different accounts, not posts. How they say it runs from 📋 copied (the same words, pasted) to 🗣️ each in their own words; folklore is retold in people's own words, while coordinated posting and copypasta are copied.
