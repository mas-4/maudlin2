Every outlet we read on enough of our stories, placed by which stories it covers: outlets close together run the same stories. Each dot is in the outlet's AllSides lean color, white when it isn't rated. The second view places them by how they word them: how close their stories and words are to the AP's and Reuters', and how heated their headlines are.

💡 The first axis isn't left against right. Left and right part on the second axis: crime and immigration stories at the bottom, stories critical of Trump at the top.

🔧 How the map is made

🗞️ Which stories it covers: a correspondence analysis of outlets × stories (did the outlet run the story or not). The two axes drawn hold {{ profile.axes.inertia[0] }}% and {{ profile.axes.inertia[1] }}% of the differences between outlets. The maths leaves each axis' direction open, so the lean ratings set it: the end where outlets rated far left or far right gather is to the right, the end where left-rated ones gather is up.

🌶️ How it words them: across, the average rank among these outlets of the share of its stories a wire also ran, how near its headlines are to the wire's on those stories, and its vocabulary; up, the average rank of its loaded words, combat verbs, sensational words, anger and labels.

🏷️ The types: Ward clusters on about fifty measures of what outlets do, each group of measures weighted alike. A type is shown when it holds together: sorted again a hundred times with a fifth of the outlets left out at random, it comes back at least three-quarters the same on average (clusterwise Jaccard). An outlet is given its type only when at least two of the three outlets it behaves most like share it, and a type named for a side only when it's rated on that side or not rated.

🧲 The stories that pull each way

An outlet that runs these stories is drawn toward that end of the map. Each story's color says whose outlets ran it more: the share of the left-rated outlets on the map that ran it against the share of the right-rated ones. Hover or tap a story for both shares; click one to open its page.

🏷️ Outlet types

Outlets sorted into types by what they do on their front pages, not by any rating: which stories they run and when, beside whom, how close to the wires, how they word their headlines, how often they rewrite them. Outlets shade into each other more than they fall into islands, so a type is shown only when it holds together each time we sort them again; among the big mainstream outlets the finer types don't, so those carry only the broad family, named for what it isn't. A "partisan web" is one side's own outlets, running the same fight-heavy stories as each other.

The most heated first: 🌶️ the bar is how heated the type's middle outlet words its headlines (its percentile among the outlets on the map); 📡 the second, how close it is to the AP and Reuters. Hover or tap a type for what it does and its outlets.
