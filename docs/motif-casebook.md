# Motif casebook: rulings with their reasons

The cases behind `docs/motif-praxis.md`: each a motif or claim the person ruled on, what the tellings said, the call,
and why. Kept as worked examples for anyone proposing filings, notes or genres, and for the models to learn the
reasoning from, not only the verdicts. Started Oct 9, 2026, from the person's session with Claude through the maudlin
MCP; newest cases go at the end of their section. Motif numbers are the index's (M###).

## Filing: the telling, not the why

**Trump has a scheduled call with Putin** (WSJ What's News; MeidasTouch playing Trump's clip "Today is Vladimir Putin's
birthday"). Was in Whose side are you on anyway? (M598) and Manchurian candidate (M550).
Ruling: taken out of Whose side. No teller questions his loyalty; the traction plainly comes from his fondness for
strongmen, but that's the person's reading, not the tellers'.
Why: "we should just evaluate the claims and not put in my own signal." Filing the why teaches the model to read
subtext into straight news.

**Trump angry at Fox News for not covering his rallies.** Was in Blame shifting, Blame the messenger, Political
retaliation.
Tellings: "ALL ABOUT ME! Trump, 80, Rages About Being Ignored"; "turned on his conservative friends at Fox News for not
fawning over him enough"; Trump: "Our people want to see our Rallies on Fox News, or they stop watching (like I am doing
right now!)"; "a Trump rally in a high school gym in Ohio… How times have changed."
Ruling: out of all three (nothing went wrong that he deflects; Fox reported no bad news; "I'll stop watching" isn't
punishment from power). Filed under Big crowds (his "packed house" argument, answered with "a high school gym") and a
new Archetype, Narcissus (M674). Claim reworded: "threatening to retaliate" became "saying he's stopped watching", the
only threat in the tellings.

## Filing: whose words carry it

**Blame the victim (M227)** became an Argument: the charge "you're blaming the victim". Kept where someone makes the
charge: the Cornell case and "a culture that often blames victims"; a woman condemned for shaming a survivor ("how
willing people are to cape for rapists"); trans people "falsely accused… vilified"; the ADHD and autism overdiagnosis
debate, where one post says the system "now victim blames them w/ accusations of overdiagnosis".
Taken out: the Cornell woman receiving threats (an event; moved to Campus sexual assault); male violence learned and
normalized (Patriarchy and the others cover it); and two claims where the tellers themselves doubt the accuser (Matt
Walsh: "she concluded she was assaulted only after she was disinvited from a Halloween party"; Clay Travis & Buck
Sexton: "The basis of her rape allegation is she didn't get invited"). Filing those under Blame the victim would put the
critics' verdict on the doubters.
Those two became a motif of their own, **Potiphar's wife** (M672, Plots): an accusation said to be invented out of
spite or regret, its timing or changes offered as proof. It rests on **He said, she said** (M073, renamed from Rape
accusation) and is related to Blame the victim: the two sides of one fight.

**Crush dissent (M309)** kept where a teller describes silencing: Celtic fans ("so outraged at a ludicrously tiny show
of dissent"); Brazil "attempted to crush the right-wing movement by imprisoning Bolsonaro"; French students, where the
government "is trying to intimidate and discredit the protesting students". Taken out: French protest claims that
report tear gas, clashes or arrests as straight news, or no crackdown at all. Clashes aren't silencing.

**The spy among us (M254)**: the RAF Fairford arrest went to Security breach. Police said "preparing terrorist acts",
not spying, and it was the seventh arrest in "the suspected Fairford base plot", a cell rather than a lone wolf.

**Proxy warfare (M224)**: kept the Yemen and Houthi claims, whose tellers name the patrons ("Iran-backed Houthi
fighters", "Saudi-backed forces", "an overspill of the US-Iran war"). Taken out: Iran closing Hormuz (Iran acting
directly) and Russia striking Kyiv as Germany sends aid (straight news; moved to The war is escalating).

**Political party surge (M249)** taken off "voter anger may help Democratic challengers": the Pod Save America host
says "there's such an anti-incumbent mood", a mood, not a party surging. New motif **Throw the bums out** (M669).

**Class conflict (M049)** taken off the Social Security claim: the tellings are old against young, not rich against
the rest. New motif **Generational theft** (M670); Some of you may die (M650) widened and applied (Matt Walsh:
boomers "know who should make the sacrifice. And the answer is their children and their grandchildren").

## Claims: wording and splitting

- **Reword when the gloss hides the shape.** The ADHD claim's summary left out the victim-blaming charge it's filed for;
  reworded from the posts. The Cornell "changing story" claim covered only one of five tellings; reworded to carry
  Walsh's and the countersuit telling too.
- **One subject told from two sides stays one claim.** Brazil: the right says the government banned X and Rumble and
  blocked conservative accounts; the left says platforms suppressed Lula's ads. The person: "both of them are about
  Brazil and censorship, I don't know if I'd split that." Reworded to carry both; filed under Crush dissent and Press
  freedom threat (the right's half) and a new Theory, **Shadowbanned** (M673), told by both.
- **Two subjects merged is a split** (not yet possible in the checker): on the voter-anger claim, the David Frum Show
  guest speaks of Democrats angry at their own leadership class, a different subject from Pod Save America's
  anti-incumbent mood.

## Genres

- **It was one of them (M201): Theory.** "The plot is what happened, the motif is what people think about it."
- **Bribing voters (M321): Plot**, though "what people think" too: "$5,000 checks if we win" is plain on its face;
  "It was one of them!" about a plain crash adds a hidden agent. Theory needs the shadowy reading, not the obvious one.
- **Corruption (M244): Theory; Graft (M330): Plot.** The system against the particular deal.
- **Rigged election (M174): Theory.**
- **Blame the victim (M227): Argument** ("it's definitely an argument because it's often brought up, an actual plot
  would need someone to identify it and it's only ever used as a rhetorical move").
- **Blame shifting (M107): Archetype**, renamed **Passing the buck** to mirror The buck stops here. Its tellers
  portray a person ("Rather than offer solutions where his zone is blame… Bankrupts the casino, blame").
- **Out of touch (M385): first a Theory**, then reconsidered under the Belief/Theory test; see below.
- **Proxy warfare (M224): Plot.** Every telling is an episode.
- **Meaningless name change (M333): Plot**, reworked as **Rebranding**: a leader renames institutions to put his stamp
  on them (the "Super Intelligence Force", the Kennedy Center, the Department of Defense).
- **Belief or Theory** (the test in the praxis): Polls are inaccurate, Politicians' empty promises, Political theater,
  End times and In this economy? became Beliefs; Cancel culture a Theory; Silent complicity a Value; Education security
  a Plot. **AI is existentially dangerous** stays a Theory: the person wasn't confident it's a Belief, and it is argued
  as a causal account rather than assumed.
- **Reward a loyal political figure (M331)** hid two motifs: the Value (a loyal ally should be rewarded; its claim,
  Jack Smith for Attorney General) and the Theory **It's her turn** (M671, a seed): a party hands the nomination to
  whoever is owed a turn.

## Names

- Hostis publicus became **The enemy of all mankind** (M637): plain over Latin.
- **Lucretia** (M675) kept for a different shape than He said, she said: Lucretia's rape brought down the Tarquins, so
  the motif is a violation that topples the powerful, not a contest over whom to believe.
- **Potiphar's wife**: the classic false-accusation motif (Genesis 39), kept with "False accuser" as the plain fallback.
- Plain idioms preferred where they fit: Throw the bums out, Passing the buck, Pain at the pump (was Complaining about
  gas prices; rests on In this economy?), He said, she said, Shadowbanned, Rebranding.

## Open questions

- Out of touch: its claims are mostly the premise "they don't get it" (older politicians and AI, a billionaire
  suspicious to working-class voters, a club board, the AI industry "tone deaf"), which reads as a Belief.
- The enemy are cowards (M349): one claim, a fake video of a crying Ukrainian soldier; needs rework, and its relation
  to The enemy of all mankind (M637).
- Should a widely shared theory used in passing be filed as a Belief?
